# Investment committee practice case

**Hypothetical acquisition; educational research only. Not transaction or investment advice.**

## Proposed practice transaction

The case acquires Energizer at a hypothetical $4,806.0m enterprise value, or 9.0x $534.0m underwritten EBITDA. Sponsor equity is $2,521.5m. New senior and junior debt total $2,403.0m, with approximately $49.9m retained finance leases at closing. The $400.5m revolver starts undrawn. The information set ends November 30, 2025; closing is assumed December 31, 2025.

## Calculated outcomes

The base case generates $639.8m exit EBITDA and $1,434.2m exit debt. At an unchanged 9.0x exit multiple, sponsor proceeds are $4,199.3m, or 1.67x MOIC and 10.7% XIRR. The return is modest relative to the operating and financing risks embedded in the case; the model does not raise exit multiples to manufacture an attractive answer.

The upside produces 2.71x MOIC and 22.0% XIRR. The downside exhausts committed liquidity and reaches a $309.5m maximum minimum-cash shortfall. Its return is unavailable without separately underwriting a restructuring, new capital or another financing solution. Those solutions are outside this model.

## Value creation

1. EBITDA growth comes from segment volume, price/mix and cash-margin assumptions, less cash operating expenses and recurring cost reserves.
2. Operating cash generation pays down senior debt. The waterfall accounts for taxes, interest, working capital and capex before optional principal repayments.
3. The base case receives no multiple-expansion benefit. Fees and retained minimum cash reduce sponsor returns.

The workbook contains an exact equity-value bridge tying initial sponsor capital to unfloored exit equity. Entry/exit sensitivity tables separate financing and valuation effects from operating-case changes.

## Main diligence questions and downside risks

- Earnings quality: management adjusted EBITDA differs from cash available for debt service. The case removes share-compensation addbacks and production-credit benefits and includes recurring cash costs.
- Cash conversion: inventory, collection periods and supplier terms can consume liquidity even while EBITDA is positive.
- Demand and margins: weaker volumes, input costs, tariff exposure and competitive pricing can impair operating cash generation.
- Execution: margin recovery and restructuring assumptions require evidence. Their benefits are not guaranteed.
- Financing: rates, fees, refinancing at par, legal debt capacity and covenant terms are hypothetical. Quarter-end borrowing conventions can miss intra-quarter peaks.
- Accounting and tax: PPA, credit collection, deferred taxes, pension claims and lease treatment require transaction-specific review.

## Practice conclusion

Under the modeled base assumptions, the business services debt and generates a positive sponsor return, but the downside does not remain funded. A real committee would require evidence on cash conversion, recurring adjustments, liquidity needs and financing terms before evaluating a transaction. This artifact supports learning that diligence process; it does not recommend a security or transaction.
